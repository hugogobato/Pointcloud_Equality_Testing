# WP5A: single-cloud observation models and estimands

This note closes the observation-model part of Phase 5. The central point is
that a point cloud is not itself a replicate. Replication comes either from
independent points inside an i.i.d. metric-measure model, from approximately
independent blocks in an increasing-window point-process model, or from an
explicit likelihood. If none of these is defensible, the output is a
deterministic discrepancy and not a p-value.

The current methods-paper scope is Regime I. The controlled generators in
`tda2s.dgp` generate independent points from a declared law, and this is the
only observation model that can currently be claimed for the synthetic
benchmark. A future spatial or ecological application must be classified
again from its data-collection process.

## 1. Common notation and the locked Regime-I target

Let \((\mathsf M,d)\) be a separable metric space with its Borel sigma field,
and let \(\mathcal D_d\) denote the space of finite persistence diagrams in
degree \(d\), with a fixed convention for discarding essential classes. Let
\(\mathsf F\) be a fixed filtration. For a probability law \(P\) on
\(\mathsf M\), define the fixed-size barcode law

\[
  \Phi^m_{0:1}(P)
  = \mathcal L\left(
    \bigl(\operatorname{Dgm}_0(\mathsf F(Y_{1:m})),
          \operatorname{Dgm}_1(\mathsf F(Y_{1:m}))\bigr)
    \right),
  \qquad Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P.
\]

The notation \(0:1\) means that the joint degree-0 and degree-1 diagram is
the object being compared. In the present lock, \(m=25\) and \(\mathsf F\)
is the Vietoris--Rips radius filtration (an edge between two points at
distance \(d\) enters at radius \(d/2\); the shared PH module reports this
radius scale for every filtration). The target is

\[
  H_{0,m}^{\mathrm{bar}}:
  \Phi^{25}_{0:1}(P_0)=\Phi^{25}_{0:1}(P_1).
\]

This is a law of barcodes generated by fixed-size i.i.d. subclouds. It is not
equality of the two observed empirical measures, equality of the full point
laws, equality of supports, or equality of an unspecified topological type.
The fixed value of $m$ is part of the estimand. Changing it changes the
question.

The strongest-simple secondary null is

\[
  H_0^{\mathrm{law}}: P_0=P_1.
\]

It is a baseline and a calibration reference, not the headline target. The
implication $H_0^{\mathrm{law}}\Rightarrow H_{0,m}^{\mathrm{bar}}$ is immediate,
whereas the converse generally fails. The witness script shows both the
coarsening and the dependence of the barcode law on $m$.

For a fixed diagram pair \(D=(D_0,D_1)\), the planned SC-B diagnostic uses a
fixed, degree-tagged persistence scale-space embedding with bandwidth
\(h=0.10\), with no median-heuristic retuning in a confirmatory analysis. The
joint kernel is the tensor product of the degree-specific exponentiated
persistence scale-space kernels. An additive or averaged degree kernel would
test only equality of the two degree marginals and could miss changes in their
dependence. The corresponding MMD is a discrepancy in the RKHS norm. The
Phase 5D note states the bounded-support conditions under which this joint
kernel is characteristic. This kernel choice does not change the definition
of \(H_{0,m}^{\mathrm{bar}}\).

The following transformations are declared nuisances for the locked
metric-invariant target: reordering points, translating, rotating, or
reflecting every point in both samples by the same ambient isometry. Density,
intensity, anisotropic deformation, boundary truncation, and global scale are
not nuisances under the raw-metric lock. If a scientific application wants
scale invariance, the filtration and target must be changed before testing.

## 2. Regime I: i.i.d. metric-measure sampling

### Probability model

For arm $a\in\{0,1\}$, the observed cloud is

\[
  C_{a,n_a}=(Y_{a,1},\ldots,Y_{a,n_a}),
  \qquad Y_{a,j}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}P_a.
\]

The two arms are independent, or are conditionally independent given the
declared experimental design. The sampling unit is an individual point, not
an overlapping subcloud. A disjoint partition into blocks of size $m$ gives

\[
  K_a=\left\lfloor\frac{n_a}{m}\right\rfloor
\]

independent barcode draws after discarding the remainder. Reusing points in
overlapping subclouds increases Monte Carlo output but does not increase
$K_a$.

The base asymptotic sequence is

\[
  n_0,n_1\to\infty,
  \qquad \frac{n_1}{n_0+n_1}\to\lambda\in(0,1),
  \qquad m=25 \text{ fixed}.
\]

For the block implementation, $K_0,K_1\to\infty$ along the same sequence.
The law $P_a$ is the object of interest. If measurement error, thinning, or
registration is present, its mechanism is an additional nuisance and must be
part of the observation model rather than absorbed into the word “point
cloud.”

### What is and is not identified

Under this model, $P_a$ is identifiable from an unbounded i.i.d. sample in
the usual weak sense, subject to the chosen measurable model class. The
finite-$m$ map $P\mapsto\Phi^m_{0:1}(P)$ is generally not injective. In
particular, it is invariant under ambient isometries and can discard density
information. Therefore a barcode-law test cannot be reported as a full
point-law test unless injectivity has been established for a restricted model
class.

The fixed-$m$ target is stable under metric perturbations through the
metric-measure stability results of Blumberg, Gal, Mandell, and Pancia. Those
results do not make overlapping subclouds independent and do not by
themselves provide a two-cloud p-value. The independent-block construction
is the source of repeated information for SC-B.

### Candidate nulls

The primary null is $H_{0,m}^{\mathrm{bar}}$. The secondary baseline is
$H_0^{\mathrm{law}}$. A support-topology null is deliberately not included:
the same support can carry different sampling densities, and those densities
can change the fixed-$m$ barcode law. A finite summary such as an integrated
persistence coordinate is a diagnostic or a separate target, not evidence for
the full barcode-law null.

## 3. Regime II: stationary or mixing point processes

### Probability model

Let \(\Xi_a\) be a simple stationary point process on \(\mathbb R^q\), with
finite positive intensity \(\lambda_a\). We observe one realization in an
increasing window $W_L=L W$, where $W\subset\mathbb R^q$ is bounded with
regular boundary and $L\to\infty$:

\[
  C_{a,L}=\Xi_a\cap W_L.
\]

The two processes are independent across arms. Within an arm there is no
point-level exchangeability assumption. A concrete validity route requires a
mixing condition, moment bounds for the chosen topological statistic, and a
block sequence $b_L\to\infty$ with $b_L/L\to0$. Blocks must be separated or
handled by a theorem that quantifies their dependence. Boundary corrections
are part of the statistic.

The sampling unit is an approximately independent spatial block. The number
of points is random and is not itself a replicate count. The asymptotic
sequence is the increasing observation window, not repeated Monte Carlo
subcloud generation inside one fixed window.

### Locked process-level target

Regime II is not the current flagship, but the target is frozen sufficiently
to prevent an implementation from silently switching to a point-level null.
Fix a finite radius grid

\[
  \mathcal R=(0.25,0.50,0.75,1.00)
\]

in the application's declared raw distance units, degrees $d\in\{0,1\}$,
and a fixed edge-corrected persistent-Betti functional (B_{a,d}(r)). The
normalized process summary is

\[
  \theta_{a,d,r}
  = \frac{1}{\lambda_a}
    \lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{|W_L|}
    \mathbb E\left[B_d(r;\mathsf F(\Xi_a\cap W_L))\right].
\]

The candidate process null is the finite-vector equality

\[
  H_0^{\mathrm{proc}}:
  \theta_0=\theta_1,
  \qquad \theta_a=(\theta_{a,d,r})_{d\in\{0,1\},r\in\mathcal R}.
\]

The intensity normalization is intentional: a change in first-order
intensity alone is not called a topological difference by this target. A
scientific question that wants intensity differences must retain an
unnormalized target. The process target does not identify the entire point
process law, arbitrary inhomogeneity, or finite-window behavior.

The foundations for this route are the point-process persistence LLN and CLT
results of Hiraoka, Shirai, and Trinh, Biscio, Chenavier, Hirsch, and Svane,
and Roycraft, Krebs, and Polonik. The latter's smoothed-bootstrap result is
for specified stabilizing finite-dimensional statistics. It is not a generic
process-level bootstrap for arbitrary diagram discrepancies.

## 4. Regime III: fixed clouds with no sampling model

Here (C_0,C_1) are two fixed finite subsets or arrays and no probability law,
observation window, or repeated-data mechanism is declared. There is no
sampling unit and no asymptotic sequence. The valid output is a deterministic
discrepancy, for example

\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{det}}(C_0,C_1)
  = \left\|\varphi_{0:1}(C_0)-\varphi_{0:1}(C_1)\right\|,
\]

where the representation and norm are fixed in advance. Stability bounds and
perturbation sensitivity are valid reporting tools. A frequentist p-value,
a posterior probability, and a population generalization claim are not
identified by the two arrays. The fixed-cloud witness in the accompanying
script makes this failure concrete by assigning positive probability to the
same observed arrays under generative laws with arbitrarily different tails.

## 5. Regime IV: explicit generative model

An explicit model declares a likelihood for the observed points, a shared or
separate parameterization, and a prior before looking at the comparison
result. A generic point-level version is

\[
  C_a\mid\vartheta_a\sim L_a(\cdot\mid\vartheta_a),
  \qquad
  M_{\mathrm{shared}}: \vartheta_0=\vartheta_1,
  \qquad
  M_{\mathrm{separate}}: \vartheta_0\ne\vartheta_1.
\]

An equivalence event must also be declared, for example

\[
  \Delta(\vartheta_0,\vartheta_1)\le\varepsilon,
\]

with a scientifically chosen margin (\varepsilon). A posterior probability
of that event or a posterior model probability is meaningful only relative to
the stated likelihood and prior. A prior does not create empirical
replication. If a persistence-diagram substitution likelihood is used, the
dependence induced among diagram points must be acknowledged; the construction
of Maroulas, Nasrin, and Oballe is a modeling candidate, not an identification
theorem for two observed clouds.

Regime IV is conditional in this project. It is not selected for the methods
paper because no domain-defensible likelihood, observation-bias mechanism,
and prior-predictive constraint is currently recorded.

## 6. Nonidentification and target-separation witnesses

The executable witness suite is `scripts/phase5_estimand_witnesses.py`. It
uses a fixed seed record, finite discrete laws, and the H0 Vietoris--Rips
barcode, for which all comparisons can be checked exactly or by exhaustive
enumeration. It tests three different failures that must not be conflated.

First, two laws can have exactly the same support and hence the same support
topology while having different masses on that support. For a four-point
square, the uniform law and a full-support nonuniform law have different
collision probabilities and different expected two-point H0 persistence. The
fixed-$m$ barcode law therefore changes even though the geometric support is
unchanged, certified at $m=2$ by exact enumeration and at the locked $m=25$
by the all-atoms-drawn lower bound. SC-A and SC-B can reject this pair; a
support-topology claim cannot be inferred from either rejection.

Second, a finite topological summary can be blind to a point-law difference.
On an equilateral three-point support, two distinct probability vectors are
chosen to have the same sum of squared masses (0.44 in both cases). Because
every two-point subcloud of the equilateral support is congruent, the m=2 H0
barcode law is determined entirely by the collision probability, so equal
squared masses give equal expected two-point H0 persistence *and* equal m=2
barcode laws on this support; the script verifies both by exact enumeration.
This support-specific equivalence is not a general implication: on a generic
support, equal expected persistence does not imply equal law. At the locked
m=25, the occupancy probabilities differ, so the fixed-$m$ barcode-law null
is false, certified by the occupancy-based total-variation lower bound. The
implication to reject is only "equal finite summary implies the barcode-law
null", and the witness is a counterexample to that. Separately, translating a
finite-support law gives distinct laws on the ambient coordinate space with
identical barcode laws for every $m$, because translation is an isometry.
This is a desired invariance of the metric target, not evidence that the full
point law was tested.

Third, for fixed clouds (C_0,C_1), let (U(C)) be the uniform law on the
observed atoms and let (Q_L) put mass at a remote point of distance (L).
The laws

\[
  P_{a,L}=(1-\epsilon)U(C_a)+\epsilon Q_L
\]

assign positive likelihood to the exact observed clouds for every (L), but
their moments and tails can be made arbitrarily different. A separate-law
model and a shared-law model can both assign positive likelihood to the same
pair of clouds. This is why Regime III cannot inherit a p-value from
subsampling, and why Regime IV must specify its likelihood before inference.

The candidates consequently have distinct claims. SC-A is a full point-law
permutation baseline under i.i.d. exchangeability and is sensitive to density
changes. SC-B targets the fixed-$m$ barcode law using genuinely disjoint
blocks and cannot detect differences in its barcode-law fiber. SC-C sees only
the frozen finite vector and can miss both law and barcode differences. SC-D
targets the process vector under mixing and increasing-window assumptions.
SC-E gives model-relative posterior quantities. A deterministic comparison is
the only honest output in Regime III.

## 7. Gate 5A conclusion and source map

The audit passes for the synthetic methods scope under Regime I. The primary
null, the secondary baseline, $m$, filtration, degrees, kernel diagnostic,
norm, and nuisance convention are frozen in
`docs/phase5_design_lock.md`. The witness script mechanically verifies that
full-law equality, fixed-$m$ barcode-law equality, and finite-summary
equality are different statements.

The conclusion is `GO: Regime I for the synthetic methods-paper benchmark`.
It is not a blanket `GO` for a spatial or ecological data set. A future
application is `PENDING CLASSIFICATION` until its sampling metadata support
Regime I, II, or IV. If the metadata support none of them, the correct result
is `KILL inference` and a deterministic discrepancy only.

The source map is deliberately limited to the sources needed by this gate.
Blumberg et al. (2014, DOI
[`10.1007/s10208-014-9201-4`](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-014-9201-4))
defines the fixed-$m$ barcode law and its metric-measure stability, but its
i.i.d. barcode calibration does not license overlapping-subcloud inference.
Chazal et al. (2015, [PMLR 37:2143--2151](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/chazal15.html))
supports subsampling risk and stability, not an automatic p-value. Fasy et al.
(2014, DOI
[`10.1214/14-AOS1252`](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-AOS1252))
supports latent-diagram confidence constructions under explicit i.i.d.
conditions, not equality testing by confidence-ball overlap. Hiraoka et al.
(2018, DOI
[`10.1214/17-AAP1371`](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-AAP1371)), Biscio et al.
(2020, DOI
[`10.1214/20-EJS1683`](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-EJS1683)), and Roycraft et
al. (2023, DOI
[`10.1214/23-AOS2277`](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-AOS2277))
support the process-level LLN, CLT, and smoothed-bootstrap route. Hahn (2012,
DOI [`10.1080/01621459.2012.688463`](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.2012.688463))
and Loh and Stein (2004,
[Statistica Sinica](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/j14n1/j14n13/j14n13.html))
provide spatial block and quadrat templates, not PH-specific validity. Maroulas,
Nasrin, and Oballe (2020, DOI
[`10.1137/19M1268719`](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1268719))
motivates the Bayesian candidate while leaving the likelihood and prior
audits to the application.

The main guardrail for the next phase is simple: no resampling method may be
implemented until its declared regime, sampling unit, and target are carried
in its result object. In particular, the number of overlapping subclouds is
never an effective sample size.
